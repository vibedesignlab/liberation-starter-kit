# Brand anatomy schema

Use this schema to convert evidence into a coherent model. Complete every applicable field, cite evidence IDs, and mark each entry `Observed` or `Inferred` with confidence.

## 1. Strategic anatomy

- Category definition: what business the brand behaves as if it is in.
- Primary audience: desired identity, situation, anxieties, and sophistication.
- Tension or enemy: convention, friction, status quo, or emotional problem opposed.
- Promise: functional, emotional, and social value.
- Reasons to believe: product truth, operations, heritage, expertise, technology, or community.
- Competitive posture: premium versus accessible, specialist versus broad, insider versus universal, expressive versus restrained.
- Cultural role: symbol, tool, curator, provocateur, host, teacher, infrastructure, or companion.
- Portfolio logic: masterbrand, endorsed products, sub-brands, collections, editions, or feature hierarchy.

End with a positioning statement and a short list of strategic tensions the system must preserve.

## 2. Verbal anatomy

- Vocabulary fields and repeated semantic territories.
- Syntax, sentence length, punctuation, fragments, and cadence.
- Point of view, pronouns, distance, certainty, and emotional temperature.
- Message hierarchy from brand belief to product proof to action.
- Naming patterns for brand, products, features, collections, colors, and content.
- CTA, onboarding, help, error, and transactional behavior where applicable.
- Humor, metaphor, cultural reference, technicality, and taboo zones.
- Channel modulation: what changes between campaign, product, UI, support, and social.

Separate quoted examples from reusable rules. Keep any quotation short and attributable.

## 3. Visual primitives

- Mark behavior: scale, clearance, placement, lockups, repetition, and omission.
- Typography: role, classification, width, contrast, optical size, weight, case, tracking, line height, and pairing logic.
- Color: semantic roles, saturation, value structure, proportion, contrast, background behavior, and exceptions.
- Shape: geometry, silhouette, corner behavior, line weight, containers, and cropping.
- Surface: border, shadow, depth, texture, grain, gloss, translucency, or material.
- Icon and illustration: stroke, fill, metaphor, perspective, detail, and animation.

Do not infer an exact font or token value from a screenshot without corroboration. Describe measured or likely traits and state uncertainty.

## 4. Composition grammar

- Grid, alignment, anchoring, and container behavior.
- Spacing rhythm, density, whitespace function, and grouping.
- Scale contrast and focal hierarchy.
- Symmetry, imbalance, overlap, crop, repetition, and interruption.
- Relationship between image, type, object, and negative space.
- Responsive or format adaptation across channels.

Write composition findings as relationships: `when X, do Y because Z`.

## 5. Image language

- Subject and product role.
- Human presence, casting, gaze, gesture, styling, and diversity.
- Setting, props, environment, and world-building.
- Framing, camera distance, lens character, angle, perspective, and depth.
- Light direction, hardness, exposure, contrast, reflection, and shadow.
- Palette, temperature, material, texture, atmosphere, and imperfection.
- Retouching, compositing, grain, typography in image, and generative artifacts.
- Motion: pace, continuity, camera movement, cuts, transitions, and loops.
- Channel variants: hero, PDP, campaign, social, editorial, tutorial, and support.

Conclude with invariants, controlled variables, prohibited treatments, and a usable shot list.

## 6. Product behavior

- Information architecture and disclosure rhythm.
- Interaction tempo and feedback intensity.
- Defaults, permissions, guidance, and error recovery.
- Personalization versus consistency.
- Product ceremony: reveal, unboxing, activation, progress, reward, or completion.
- Service behavior before, during, and after purchase.

Do not project digital behavior onto a physical-only brand without evidence. Mark proposed digital behavior as `Transferred`.

## 7. System synthesis

Produce:

1. Five to eight grammar-kernel rules.
2. Three productive tensions that keep the brand distinctive.
3. Three surface signatures that must not be copied.
4. Exceptions and scope limits.
5. Evidence IDs and confidence for every item.

A grammar-kernel rule must explain an input, a transformation, and an intended effect. Reject adjective-only conclusions.

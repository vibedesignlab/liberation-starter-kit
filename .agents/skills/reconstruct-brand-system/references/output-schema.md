# Output schema

Use `assets/brand-dossier.md` as the drafting skeleton. Preserve the order below so later reviewers and scripts can find required sections.

## Required front matter

- Case title
- Reference brand and scope
- Fictional target product
- Market, channels, language, and era
- Transfer intensity
- Research and capture dates
- Assumptions
- Evidence coverage summary

## Required sections

### 1. Executive synthesis

State the opportunity, target-product positioning, and the five-to-eight-rule grammar kernel. Make the relationship to the source legible without relying on visual imitation.

### 2. Evidence and scope

Summarize source tiers, channel and visual-category coverage, blind spots, exclusions, and confidence. Link the source manifest and contact sheet when local artifacts exist.

### 3. Source brand anatomy

Cover strategy, verbal behavior, visual primitives, composition, image language, and product behavior. Attach evidence IDs and labels to material claims.

### 4. Positioning for the fictional product

Include category frame, audience, tension, promise, proof, competitive distinction, positioning statement, value pillars, and productive tensions.

### 5. Verbal branding guidelines

Include voice principles, tone modulation, lexicon, syntax, message hierarchy, naming logic, do/don't pairs, and representative target-native copy.

### 6. Visual branding guidelines

Include identity behavior, typography traits and roles, color logic, composition, shape, surface, icon or illustration, motion, channel modulation, and do/don't rules.

### 7. Foundational design-token direction

Include typography, color, spacing, grid, shape, elevation, icon, and motion semantics. Mark each literal value `directional`, `tested`, or `validated`. State accessibility and responsive constraints.

### 8. Brand and product image direction

Include invariants, channel variants, subject, casting, setting, composition, camera and lens character, lighting, palette, materials, post-production, motion, negative direction, and a shot matrix.

### 9. Source-to-target mapping

Summarize the mapping matrix. Explain rejected source traits and every medium copy-risk decision.

### 10. Application examples

Apply the system to at least three distinct contexts. Show which grammar rules generated each decision.

### 11. Do / Don't

Use paired, operational examples. Avoid vague preference statements.

### 12. Confidence, gaps, and validation plan

List low-confidence conclusions, missing evidence, product tests, accessibility checks, user validation, legal review needs, and decisions that remain directional.

### 13. Evidence index

List cited evidence IDs with titles and paths or URLs. Keep the full source metadata in the manifest.

## Writing rules

- Lead with conclusions, then show the evidence chain.
- Use short quotations only when wording itself is evidence.
- Distinguish source facts from target recommendations visually and linguistically.
- Make every guideline executable by a designer, writer, photographer, or product team.
- Replace adjective stacks with relationships, constraints, examples, and counterexamples.
- Avoid claiming exact token values or font identities without corroboration and testing.

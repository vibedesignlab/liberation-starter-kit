# Transfer rules

Translate the source brand's causal logic into a new product system without reproducing source-owned signatures.

## Contents

1. Choose the transfer intensity
2. Separate four layers
3. Complete the mapping matrix
4. Resolve target conflicts
5. Build positioning from the target
6. Translate verbal grammar
7. Translate visual grammar
8. Direct tokens semantically
9. Maintain originality distance
10. Test coherence

## 1. Choose the transfer intensity

- `principle-only`: transfer strategic tensions and behavioral principles; allow a visually distant result.
- `balanced`: transfer the grammar kernel and compositional logic while changing assets, category conventions, and recognizable signatures. Use by default.
- `close-study`: preserve more rhythm and expressive contrast for an explicit study, while still prohibiting logos, slogans, proprietary type treatments, trade dress, and near-identical hero compositions.

Record the selected intensity in the case brief and dossier.

## 2. Separate four layers

For each source pattern, identify:

1. `Signal`: what is visibly or verbally present.
2. `Function`: what the signal accomplishes for comprehension, emotion, trust, status, or behavior.
3. `Invariant`: the reusable relationship that produces the function.
4. `Expression`: the source-specific asset or treatment.

Transfer the invariant. Redesign the expression for the target product.

## 3. Complete the mapping matrix

Use `assets/mapping-matrix.csv` and complete:

- `mapping_id`
- `source_signal`
- `evidence_ids`
- `semantic_function`
- `grammar_rule`
- `target_constraint`
- `translated_expression`
- `transfer_intensity`
- `copy_risk`
- `confidence`
- `validation_needed`

Reject a row when the translated expression can be produced only by copying the source asset rather than applying the grammar rule.

## 4. Resolve target conflicts

When source grammar conflicts with the target product, preserve the semantic function and redesign the mechanism. Resolve conflicts in this order:

1. Product truth, accessibility, safety, and usability.
2. Target audience and category comprehension.
3. Grammar-kernel fidelity.
4. Channel constraints.
5. Surface resemblance.

Explain every discarded or altered source rule. A faithful transfer may intentionally look less similar when the category changes.

## 5. Build positioning from the target

Do not swap nouns into the source brand's positioning statement. Reconstruct:

- Target category frame.
- Specific audience tension.
- Product promise.
- Believable proof.
- Competitive distinction.
- Cultural role.

Then use source grammar to shape how the positioning is expressed, prioritized, and dramatized.

## 6. Translate verbal grammar

Keep semantic behavior such as compression, certainty, technicality, intimacy, or pacing. Replace distinctive vocabulary, slogans, proprietary names, and memorable sentence structures.

Provide target-native examples for brand belief, product value, feature proof, CTA, onboarding, empty state, error, and support when applicable.

## 7. Translate visual grammar

Translate relationships rather than literal assets:

- Scale contrast, not the exact typeface.
- Saturation budget and color roles, not sampled brand colors.
- Spacing rhythm, not traced layouts.
- Shape hierarchy, not a proprietary silhouette.
- Subject and production logic, not a recreated campaign frame.
- Transition intent, not a copied motion sequence.

## 8. Direct tokens semantically

Name token roles around meaning and use. Express first-pass values as hypotheses, ratios, or ranges. Validate them in actual target-product components before calling them final.

Include accessibility constraints, fallback typography, content expansion, localization, responsive behavior, and reduced motion where relevant.

## 9. Maintain originality distance

Flag `copy_risk` as:

- `low`: abstract strategy or relationship with a distinct expression.
- `medium`: recognizable structural resemblance that needs redesign or contextual justification.
- `high`: logo, slogan, unique naming, proprietary typography, trade dress, signature composition, or near-duplicate campaign execution.

Replace all `high`-risk translations. Review `medium` rows in the final audit.

## 10. Test coherence

Check the target system in at least three contrasting applications, such as landing hero, product-detail or core interface, and social or campaign unit. The same grammar should produce related but non-identical outputs.

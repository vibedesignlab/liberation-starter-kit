# Verbal and visual taxonomy

Use the relevant sections while extracting executable rules. Treat the dimensions as prompts, not a checklist that forces nonexistent traits.

## Verbal grammar

Analyze paired axes and locate channel-specific shifts:

- Direct ↔ suggestive
- Declarative ↔ conversational
- Technical ↔ sensory
- Sparse ↔ explanatory
- Institutional ↔ personal
- Earnest ↔ playful
- Timeless ↔ topical
- Inclusive ↔ insider-coded
- Calm ↔ urgent
- Certain ↔ exploratory

For each conclusion, capture:

- Lexicon: preferred nouns, verbs, modifiers, banned clichés, and proprietary terms.
- Syntax: average shape, fragments, parallelism, questions, lists, punctuation, and capitalization.
- Rhetoric: proof, contrast, analogy, challenge, invitation, instruction, or story.
- Cadence: compressed, measured, accelerating, repetitive, or episodic.
- Message architecture: belief → benefit → proof → action.
- Modulation: campaign, product detail, UI, support, social, and legal contexts.

Translate findings into `do`, `avoid`, and paired examples written for the target product. Never reuse a source slogan or distinctive sentence pattern verbatim.

## Typography grammar

Analyze roles before font names:

- Display, title, body, label, data, code, caption, and utility roles.
- Serif or sans classification, contrast, width, aperture, terminals, geometry, and historical tone.
- Case, weight, optical size, tracking, line height, measure, and alignment.
- Scale ratio and contrast between roles.
- Relationship to imagery, objects, and whitespace.

Propose target typefaces from required traits and licensing constraints. If no product surface has been tested, express scale and spacing as ratios or ranges.

## Color grammar

Analyze role, proportion, and interaction:

- Canvas, surface, text, muted text, border, brand anchor, accent, signal, success, warning, and error.
- Hue family, value ladder, saturation budget, temperature, and neutral bias.
- Dominant/support/accent proportions.
- Contrast and accessibility requirements.
- Photography-to-interface color relationship.

Do not reduce a color system to sampled hex values. Explain why and where each role appears.

## Spacing and layout grammar

Analyze:

- Base unit and scale behavior: linear, modular, or contextual.
- Micro, component, section, and page spacing.
- Density modes and responsive changes.
- Grid columns, margins, gutters, containers, and alignment anchors.
- Grouping, rhythm, interruption, and intentional voids.

Propose semantic tokens such as `space-inline-control`, `space-stack-section`, or equivalent project conventions before hard-coded numbers.

## Shape, depth, and icon grammar

Analyze corner hierarchy, container shapes, line weights, border contrast, shadow softness, depth levels, icon stroke or fill, metaphor type, detail, and optical corrections. Link every primitive to a behavioral or emotional function.

## Motion grammar

Analyze duration families, easing character, staging, continuity, anticipation, overshoot, parallax, camera behavior, and reduced-motion behavior. Describe motion as state change and intent, not decorative adjectives.

## Image production grammar

Specify:

- Subject hierarchy and product prominence.
- Casting, wardrobe, gesture, gaze, and emotional register.
- Location, set construction, props, negative space, and environmental realism.
- Shot size, angle, camera height, perspective, lens character, depth of field, and motion blur.
- Key-to-fill relationship, source size, light direction, reflection, shadow density, and time of day.
- Palette, material response, surface condition, grain, retouching, compositing, and typography integration.
- Output format, crop family, safe areas, sequence logic, and channel use.
- Negative direction: cliché, lighting, composition, materials, demographic tokenism, and unwanted generative artifacts.

Convert the direction into a shot matrix covering brand hero, product hero, detail, use context, human story, and system or process views when applicable.

## Foundational token direction

Produce semantic roles and relationships for:

- Typography: families or traits, roles, weights, scale ratios, line height, tracking, measure.
- Color: semantic roles, tonal ladders, state colors, contrast targets, image relationships.
- Spacing: base unit hypothesis, scale, component density, section rhythm.
- Grid: containers, columns, gutters, breakpoints, alignment anchors.
- Shape: radius, border, icon, and control hierarchy.
- Elevation: layers, shadow or border strategy, overlays.
- Motion: durations, easing, sequence, feedback, reduced motion.

Label literal values `directional`, `tested`, or `validated`. Do not call an untested direction a finished design system.

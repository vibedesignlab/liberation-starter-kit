# Evidence and visual-layer model

## Source hierarchy

- `primary`: official site, product, packaging, interface, press kit, campaign, store, social account, report, manual, or first-party interview.
- `authoritative-secondary`: credited agency/designer case study, named interview, reputable design or business publication, or archive.
- `contextual`: retailer, reviewer, community, resale, or user material used only for reception and real-world context.

Use primary evidence for current official expression. Secondary evidence may clarify authorship or intent. Contextual repetition never converts speculation into brand fact.

## Required evidence strata

Cover applicable strata independently:

| Layer | Evidence examples | It cannot prove by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy and portfolio | about, catalog, pricing, reports, launches | exact visual rules |
| Verbal system | headlines, naming, UI, packaging, support | photography or product form |
| Identity and channel tokens | marks, type, color, grids, web/app shell | total brand mood or product CMF |
| Key visual | identity-to-UI/content translation, campaign systems, graphic devices, motion, cross-channel templates | a brand-wide visual grammar from one campaign or photograph |
| Brand mood/world | editorial, spatial, cultural, environmental evidence | exact design tokens |
| Photography and film | portraits, environments, motion, human stories | intrinsic object geometry |
| Product representation | PDP, packshot, UI mockup, screen recording, detail, use, and factual demonstration | the physical or digital product independent of presentation |
| Product-native visual and cognitive language | physical views and states; actual UI structure, states, motion, and behavior | photography, mockup styling, or campaign art direction |
| Packaging and unboxing | packs, inserts, hierarchy, opening sequence | the complete service system |
| Interface and behavior | screens, states, guidance, error/recovery | physical-product behavior without evidence |
| Space and service | store, signage, care, repair, support | universal architecture from one collaborator |

## Five visual distinctions

### 1. Identity and channel tokens

Study roles and relations: typography, color, spacing, grid, shapes, icons, elevation, motion, responsive behavior. Website tokens are one owned-digital implementation, not the whole brand.

Preserve exact values as dated channel evidence when they are directly observable. At synthesis, convert repeated evidence into portable roles and relationships rather than a prescribed implementation token set. Keep brand color scheme, typography hierarchy, spacing strategy, and layout strategy usable across different design systems, platforms, and locales.

### 2. Key visual

Study the repeatable visual operating grammar that translates brand identity into UI, content design, campaign systems, imagery, and motion. Record the core visual premise; semantic focal actor; typography-message-image relationship; color-layer roles; shape, graphic device, layering, and material logic; grid, scale, spacing, and composition; UI/component translation; content/editorial template translation; imagery role; motion behavior; format adaptation; invariants; variables; authorship; lifespan; and exceptions. Separate masterbrand relationships from title, collaborator, season, product launch, and channel-specific art.

The final report must show both the operating rule and its cross-surface applications. Use a structured operating model and at least six direct EV images across three or more surface roles such as identity, UI, content/campaign, imagery, and motion. A campaign photograph, product-photo sequence, contact sheet, mood board, adjective list, or camera specification cannot substitute for this section. A content sequence may be added only after the cross-surface grammar is established.

### 3. Brand mood and world

Study atmosphere across environments and channels: cultural posture, emotional temperature, material imagination, casting, settings, sound/motion, and reality-versus-fiction boundary. Do not reduce mood to adjectives; tie it to production causes.

### 4. Photography, film, and product representation

Study how products are presented: shot role, camera distance/height, perspective, light, set, props, casting, palette, retouching, compositing, crop, device frame, UI mockup styling, screen capture, motion, and channel use. Separate brand/editorial photography from PDP imagery, interface demonstrations, product-in-use, factual explanation, and service imagery.

### 5. Product-native visual and cognitive language

Study the product independent of how marketing presents it. Use one shared model for physical, digital, and hybrid products:

- category archetype or mental model;
- hierarchy, structure, boundaries, containers, and density;
- controls, affordances, navigation, states, feedback, error, and recovery;
- family invariants, controlled variants, onboarding, use, and continuity.

For physical products, add silhouette, massing, proportion, geometry, CMF, seams, ports, joints, movement, care, storage, and repair. For digital products, add information architecture, screen anatomy, component geometry, content framing, state model, motion, personalization, accessibility, localization, and cross-device continuity. For hybrid products, trace the link between physical control, digital state, service, and feedback.

Physical photography may exaggerate or hide product form. A styled screenshot or device mockup may exaggerate or hide the actual interface structure and interaction model. Confirm product-native claims across multiple real states and neutral, technical, or in-product evidence.

## Corpus rules

- Coverage before volume. Standard studies start at 24 direct local visuals; expanded studies use 40–60 only when era, market, or product-system variation requires it.
- Avoid more than roughly one quarter from one campaign/collaborator/season unless scope demands it.
- Preserve source URL, capture date, era, market, channel, creator/agency, rights note, hash, dimensions, and related variants.
- Use EV IDs permanently; exclude rather than recycle.
- Build an overview plus only useful layer sheets for identity/key visual, mood/world, photography/film, product representation, product-native language, interface/behavior, space/service, and motion.

## Research methods

Use search, direct HTTP, official downloads, feeds, APIs, archives, and static local inspection. Never start browser automation without explicit user permission.

# ST2-BRAND-MOOD-FRONT-02 — Strict frontal infusion

## Decision package

- Web role: primary brand-mood anchor for the provenance/process section.
- Information goal: make MORA's infusion craft immediately readable through one frontal, symmetrical food-making action.
- Viewpoint: strict 100% frontal, straight-on, eye-level horizontal frame.
- Physical execution: the same maker, room, bowl, pitcher, ingredients and continuous pour used in the companion side-profile image.
- Dramatic device: one motivated band of directional daylight and the long, controlled shadows it creates—not darkness, vintage styling or theatrical props.

## Final generation prompt

Use Image 1 only as the identity, wardrobe, workspace, utensil, ingredient and material-continuity reference. Recompose the scene into a new strict frontal camera position.

Use case: photorealistic-natural. Horizontal brand-mood editorial photograph for a premium infused Greek-yogurt landing page. The scene is a minimal contemporary daylight food atelier: pale limewashed back wall, one light-oak shelf parallel to the wall, clean stainless worktop parallel to the image plane, sparse food-safe tools, no decorative clutter. It must feel current, exact and edible—not nostalgic, rustic, pharmaceutical, perfumery-like or laboratory-themed.

Strict camera geometry: 100% frontal, perfectly straight-on, eye level. The camera sensor plane is parallel to the back wall and workbench. The lens axis is perpendicular to the back wall. The back edge and front edge of the worktop run horizontally across the image with no diagonal convergence. The maker stands centered and square to the camera, both shoulders equally visible and symmetrical, torso facing directly forward. Do not show a three-quarter turn. Do not place the camera to either side. Do not use an oblique shelf or diagonal table.

Subject and action: the same real adult female food maker from Image 1, with the same tied-back hair and charcoal cotton shirt/apron, looks down at the work. Her hands, the clear glass pitcher, the thin amber thyme-honey infusion stream and the wide ceramic bowl of very dense white strained yogurt form one centered vertical action. One hand pours while the other steadies the bowl. Both arms and shoulders read clearly from the front. The continuous stream must visibly enter the bowl; the yogurt holds dense folds and a shallow amber trace. No packaged product and no text.

Composition: disciplined frontal symmetry with generous negative space around the maker. Keep the maker, pitcher, stream and bowl in the central 45% of the frame. Use the shelf and worktop as strict horizontal anchors. The scene feels dramatic because a hard-but-broad window light from frame left cuts across the frontal geometry, shaping the face, hands, glass and yogurt with long controlled shadows. Preserve readable shadow detail, natural skin tone and white-yogurt highlight texture—no crushed blacks and no clipped whites.

Photography and physical realism: realistic editorial food-process photography, natural 40–50 mm perspective, no ultra-wide distortion, subtle photographic grain and invisible retouching. Prioritize believable hand anatomy and grip, glass thickness and refraction, liquid continuity, gravity, bowl contact, yogurt viscosity, steel brushing, cloth weave and one coherent light/shadow direction. The result should feel restrained, modern and cinematic without resembling a fashion pose or CGI render.

Avoid: three-quarter angle, diagonal perspective, tilted camera, asymmetrical shoulders, looking into camera, smiling pose, alternate person, alternate outfit, multiple people, rustic kitchen, 1980s office or kitchen styling, dark perfume campaign, Le Labo references, pharmacy, apothecary, beakers, pipettes, medicine bottles, specimen labels, black industrial cabinetry, moody underexposure, colored gels, excessive props, fake steam, splashes, floating utensils, plastic skin, procedural food texture, distorted hands, duplicate fingers, disconnected stream, CGI cleanliness, text, logo, watermark.

## Observable checks

- The frame is measurably straight-on: wall, shelf and worktop are parallel to the image plane.
- Both shoulders are equally visible; the maker is not in a three-quarter pose.
- Pitcher, stream and bowl form one centered, physically connected action.
- Directional daylight creates drama while food, skin and shadow detail remain legible.
- The maker, workspace, action and materials can be matched directly to the side-profile companion.
- No fragrance, pharmacy, vintage or rustic cue appears.

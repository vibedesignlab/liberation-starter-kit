# ST2-PRODUCT-HERO-BATCH-RECORD-R10

## Role

Landing-page hero and product-truth image for a premium craft Greek yogurt brand. Photorealistic commercial food photography with physically plausible transparent glass, paper, spoon contact and dense strained-yogurt texture.

## Generation prompt

Create a 3:2 landscape commercial photograph for MORA Thyme Honey Greek yogurt. The main subject sits in the right third; keep the left 30–35% as a continuous quiet graphite copy-safe field with no objects, seams or text.

Show one open, low-wide, straight-sided transparent 150 g glass yogurt jar at a natural 30–35 degree viewing angle. The vessel must be unmistakably made for spooning food: body width about 1.5 times body height, a very wide mouth about 85% of the body diameter, no neck, no shoulders, no perfume-bottle silhouette. Preserve realistic glass thickness, refraction, rim highlights and base weight.

The jar contains dense cultured-milk-white strained Greek yogurt. At least 60–70% of the edible product remains visible through unobstructed glass. Show one restrained translucent amber thyme-honey fold continuing from the sidewall into a real spoon furrow, plus a few actual-scale thyme flecks. No honey pool, no decorative herb sprig, no smooth CGI swirl. A brushed-stainless dessert spoon rests in physical contact with the open rim and leaves one firm, broken-edge furrow that proves the yogurt's density.

Attach one partial, slightly fibrous uncoated off-white paper Batch Record label occupying less than 35% of the jar circumference and leaving most food exposed. It is a food-production record, not a perfume label. Use a disciplined technical sans-serif hierarchy. Only these larger words may be readable: “MORA”, “THYME HONEY”, “GREEK YOGURT”, “150 g”. Include compact field labels “RECIPE”, “MILK LOT”, “CULTURED”, “STRAINED”, “FOLDED”, “PACKED”, “KEEP COLD”, and “NET WT”, with at most two genuine maker check marks or initials. Keep regulatory information in a separate small standardized block. Do not invent other marketing copy.

Place the jar on a clean brushed-stainless work surface inside a dark graphite food manufactory. The non-contact background may show soft-focus sealed concrete and aged brick, but all food-contact surfaces are pristine and hygienic. Light the product with neutral 4500–5000 K task light for color accuracy while allowing the surrounding room to fall into deep graphite shadow. Preserve shadow detail, paper fibers, food color, transparent glass and believable contact shadows. Premium through restraint, material truth and work evidence—not glamour.

Camera and optics: full-frame product photography, 70–90 mm equivalent lens, realistic perspective, medium-close framing, f/8–f/11 for the entire jar and label plane to remain sharp, restrained depth falloff only behind the product, low ISO, natural micro-contrast, no bloom or synthetic sharpening.

## Composition lock

- 3:2 landscape.
- One jar only, fully inside frame, right third.
- Left 30–35% continuous dark copy-safe zone.
- Open very-wide mouth and spoon access visibly clear.
- 60–70% or more edible product visibility.
- Partial paper record label; never a full wrap.
- Neutral product light separated from the dark ambient room.

## Avoid

Narrow neck, shoulder bottle, perfume proportion, apothecary bottle, dropper, pipette, beaker, pharmacy trade dress, full-wrap white label, Ingredient + Number naming, city/date ritual, Le Labo-like wordmark, kraft nostalgia, pink or floral femininity, home kitchen, breakfast spread, whole fruit abundance, honey drizzle spectacle, fake condensation, plastic-looking glass, floating spoon, oversmoothed yogurt, CGI swirl, uniform beige color grading, crushed blacks, illegible gibberish as dominant label copy, extra jars, extra logos, hands, people.

## Acceptance checks

1. The silhouette reads as a spoonable yogurt jar before it reads as luxury packaging.
2. Food occupies the majority of the visible glass area and retains true ivory, amber and thyme tones.
3. Batch Record communicates food-native process evidence without copying perfume trade dress.
4. Spoon contact and furrow look physically plausible.
5. The left copy-safe zone survives a landing-page headline overlay.

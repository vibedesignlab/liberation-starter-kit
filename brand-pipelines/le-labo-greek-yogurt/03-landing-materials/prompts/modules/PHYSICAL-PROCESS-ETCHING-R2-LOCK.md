# MORA Physical Process Etching R2 Lock

## Purpose

Use this module for every current MORA process or ingredient etching. The drawing is editorial evidence of a physically possible food process. It may simplify a real sequence, but it may not replace the sequence with metaphor.

## Causal scene grammar

Every frame must make this chain readable without captions:

`real raw ingredient → food-safe tool → one physical action → true intermediate → finished yogurt trace`

Objects share one continuous work surface or one vertically plausible apparatus. Transfer points touch, pouring follows gravity, filters sit between source and collector, dosing tools meet the receiving food, and the final fold or spoon cut visibly retains the intermediate. Do not use arrows, labels or panels to repair an unclear scene.

## Series surface lock

- Background: one perfectly flat Cultured Cream `#F5F1E8`; no texture, aging, stain, gradient or vignette.
- Ink: Carbon `#171714` only, with fine copperplate contour, material-following hatch and restrained stipple.
- Light logic: one upper-left engraving light; lower-right surfaces carry denser hatch.
- Occupancy: 17–38% factual object group; at least 62% uninterrupted background; 8% crop safety.
- Text: none inside the artwork.
- Narrative aspect: 3:2. Ingredient process aspect: 1:1.

## Physical rejection gate

Reject when an ingredient becomes a landscape, thread, river, portal or decorative vapor; when a utensil floats; when raw, intermediate and final states are disconnected vignettes; when a vessel resembles perfume, tincture, medicine or whiskey; or when the depicted action cannot produce the shown intermediate. Reject plastic packages and paper labels.

## Background consistency gate

Sample empty background at five points outside shadows and linework. Target `#F5F1E8`. Reject if a frame differs from target by ΔE00 > 3 or from the ten-frame series median by ΔE00 > 2.5. Correct background only; preserve linework and composition.

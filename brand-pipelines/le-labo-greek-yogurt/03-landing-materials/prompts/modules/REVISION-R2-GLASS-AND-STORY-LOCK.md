# MORA Revision R2 — Glass and Story Lock

## Revision purpose

This lock applies to every asset revised after the user's eight-point review. It supersedes any older instruction that asks for polymer packaging, a plastic cup, a bulky closure, an abstract process morph, inconsistent cream paper, crowded ingredients, an occupied Hero, or an ordinary row-of-bowls laboratory display.

## Narrative roles

- **Hero / ST3-HERO-EMPTY-ATELIER-40:** an unoccupied contemporary Greek-yogurt atelier. The whole manufacturing system is visible: cloth straining, ingredient infusion, low-shear folding, glass filling and batch recording. Zero people, hands, silhouettes, reflections or human shadows.
- **Why MORA / ST3-WHY-MORA-MAKER-41:** exactly one generic MORA maker, not a founder portrait, performing a visible comparison of one cultured base in three controlled states. The person's action explains why measured infusion and folding matter.
- **Process etchings / ST3-ETCH-R2-42—51:** factual physical scenes. Each image must show a readable sequence of real ingredient, real food-safe tool, one action, the actual intermediate state and the resulting yogurt trace. No surreal morph, portal, alchemy or symbolic substitution.
- **Material Method / ST3-METHOD-R2-52—55:** food R&D pilot-bench evidence. Stainless process plates, perforated frames, filter cloth, calibrated pipettes or syringes without needles, viscosity drawdown cards, timers and batch marks may appear only when they have a clear food-process role. No decorative lab perfume props.
- **Vessel master / ST3-VESSEL-GLASS-R2-56—58:** the sole geometry authority for all later product and context images.

## Shared color and surface

- Plain-background and etching assets use one visually flat Cultured Cream field: `#F5F1E8`.
- Etching ink is Carbon `#171714`; paper has no aging, stain, edge, fiber variation, gradient or vignette.
- For the etching series, target the sampled background to `#F5F1E8`; reject a frame when its background differs from the target by more than ΔE00 3 or the series median by more than ΔE00 2.5.
- Photographic assets retain natural material color but neutralize any sepia, amber apothecary cast or cool clinical blue.

## Glass Vessel Record

- One premium spoonable-food vessel: optically clear soda-lime or flint glass, cylindrical body, short rounded shoulder, wide mouth equal to 70–75% of body diameter, thick wall and visibly heavy base.
- The glass must show edge thickness, believable refraction, soft transmitted highlights and controlled reflections. It must never show a molded polymer flange, disposable-cup rim, injection seam, acrylic haze or flexible-wall deformation.
- Closure: flush graphite anodized aluminum cap, visible height 4–6% of total closed-vessel height, diameter aligned with the glass shoulder, edge radius 0.8–1.5 mm. The cap is thin and mechanically plausible, never a tall screw block.
- The peel-back foil is the actual tamper seal beneath the cap. Do not turn foil into a decorative top disk.
- Front identity is neutral-black direct print on glass: `MORA` plus the exact recipe name only. No paper label, sticker, sleeve, carton, perfume grid, formula number or source-brand mark.
- Product is clearly spoonable Greek yogurt, not fragrance, medicine, drink, tincture or whiskey.

## Product-series geometry

- Front PDP view: exact 0° azimuth, camera square to the direct-printed face, near eye level with a subtle 8–12° downward elevation only when needed to reveal the cap edge.
- Centerline x 50%; closed-vessel bounding box x 35–65%, y 18–80%; baseline y 80%; background/ground is seamless `#F5F1E8`.
- Lock silhouette, glass thickness, cap height, camera height, centerline, baseline, occupancy, light direction and background across every SKU. Variation is limited to recipe name and the ingredient-caused yogurt trace.

## Material Method composition

- The complete object group occupies no more than 45% of frame area; at least 55% is uninterrupted background.
- Each ingredient or sample vessel occupies no more than 10–12% of frame width. Minimum clear gap between neighboring objects is 6% of frame width.
- Do not arrange six equal bowls in a row. Use one physically coherent experimental rig per frame: controlled steep-time ladder, cloth-permeability test, viscosity drawdown, or low-shear fold comparison.
- Objects must remain small enough that their relation, interval and empty field read before individual texture.

## Photographic behavior

- Photorealistic commercial food/process photography with natural microtexture and invisible retouching.
- One coherent soft key from upper-left; restrained frontal fill; grounded contact and occlusion shadows; plausible glass refraction and metal reflections; highlight detail retained.
- No CGI-like uniform gloss, cloned symmetry, floating utensils, impossible liquid meniscus, impossible reflections, HDR clarity, dramatic bokeh or theatrical smoke.

## Universal rejection rules

Reject any frame containing a plastic cup or case, paper label, bulky cap, narrow perfume neck, atomizer, dropper, apothecary amber bottle, vintage laboratory fantasy, rustic kitchen nostalgia, decorative ingredient scattering, generated logos beyond permitted direct print, pseudo-copy, watermark, inconsistent cream background, unexplained tool, or process action that cannot physically produce the depicted intermediate.
